MEDICATION GUIDE: IBUPROFEN ORAL

1. USES:
Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). It is used to relieve pain from various conditions such as headache, dental pain, menstrual cramps, muscle aches, or arthritis. It is also used to reduce fever.

2. DOSAGE & ADMINISTRATION:
- Adults and children 12 years and older: Take 200 mg to 400 mg every 4 to 6 hours as needed while symptoms persist.
- Do not exceed 1200 mg in 24 hours unless directed by a doctor.
- Take with food or milk if stomach upset occurs.

3. CONTRAINDICATIONS & BLACK BOX WARNINGS:
- Cardiovascular Risk: NSAIDs may cause an increased risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, myocardial infarction (heart attack), and stroke. Do not use right before or after heart bypass surgery.
- Gastrointestinal Risk: NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious gastrointestinal adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines.
- Contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to ibuprofen or aspirin, severe heart failure, or severe renal impairment.

4. DRUG INTERACTIONS:
- Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin, Aspirin): Concomitant use increases the risk of serious GI bleeding.
- ACE inhibitors and Diuretics: Ibuprofen may diminish the antihypertensive effect of these drugs.
- Lithium & Methotrexate: Ibuprofen may increase blood levels and toxicity of these agents.
- Alcohol: Chronic alcohol use combined with ibuprofen increases the risk of stomach bleeding.
